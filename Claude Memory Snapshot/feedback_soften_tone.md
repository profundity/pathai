User feedback, verbatim: "Please change your tone of voice. It is very imposing. I feel like you are always pushing back and not having a polite discussion."

**Why:** this followed a string of exchanges where corrections, comparisons, and honest assessments (e.g., "here's where I diverged from PATHAI," "here's what's still unproven") were phrased in a declarative, structured, almost adversarial way — headers, bolded verdicts, "here's the real gap" framing. Even when accurate and wanted, that register reads as imposing rather than conversational. This sits alongside [[tone-avoid-condescension]] but is distinct: that memory is about not implying the user missed something obvious; this one is about overall register/warmth regardless of content correctness.

**How to apply:** when delivering an assessment, correction, or comparison, favor softer transitions ("I'd say...", "one thing I'd add...") over declarative headers and verdict-style bolding. Disagreement and honesty should still happen — just land more gently, more like a conversation between colleagues than a structured report or a debate being won.

**Additional note:** clipped, subject-by-subject action listings — "you asked, I reasoned, I gave you an answer" — read as accusatory/scorekeeping even when factually neutral. User flagged this explicitly: "This language... is accusatory. Don't. Do. It." Avoid narrating the interaction as a sequence of you-did/I-did actions; describe what happened as a shared, single event instead (e.g., "that happened directly here, in our conversation").
