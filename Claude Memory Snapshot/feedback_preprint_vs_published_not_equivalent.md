**The rule:** when a paper has both a preprint (e.g. bioRxiv) and a published, peer-reviewed version, do not treat the preprint's content — text, figures, supplementary material, figure numbering — as equivalent to the published version. Verify against the actual published source being cited; if only the preprint is accessible, say so explicitly rather than quietly substituting it.

**Why:** In the PATHAI AKT/melanoma case (PMID 37931033, published in *Molecular Cancer Therapeutics*, March 2024), a bioRxiv preprint of the same underlying work was posted in August 2022 — an 18-month gap. Two concrete signals that real revision happened in between: (1) the title changed between versions — the preprint was "Genetic silencing of AKT induces melanoma cell death," the published version added "...via mTOR Suppression," suggesting the mTOR mechanism was developed further during peer review; (2) that time gap is long enough to typically involve added experiments, new controls, or even a prior rejection/resubmission elsewhere. Supplementary figure numbering and content are especially likely to shift during revision, so a preprint's "Figure S1" may not contain the same data as the published paper's "Supplementary Figure S1."

**How to apply:** treat a preprint as a *lead*, not a substitute — useful for checking whether it's even worth pursuing, but any claim, figure, or number pulled from it must be labeled "from preprint version, unconfirmed against published version" until cross-checked. This is a specific case of [[feedback-verification-then-present-ambiguity]] and [[feedback-paper-lookup-order]] — the discipline of verifying against the actual cited source rather than a plausible-looking stand-in.
